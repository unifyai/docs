title: "Watching & taking control"
description: "See your assistant's live desktop in a Meet call — and drive it yourself"
---

Everything your assistant does on its computer can happen in front of you.
During a [Unify Meet](/communication/unify-meet) call you can open a live
view of its desktop, watch it work in real time, and — when you want hands
on the wheel — take over its mouse and keyboard yourself.

## Showing the assistant's screen

In the Meet call toolbar, next to the control for sharing *your* screen,
there's a computer button: **Show assistant screen**.

Click it and the call's main view switches from your assistant's avatar to
its **live desktop** — the actual machine, streaming as it works. You'll see
pages load, text being typed, files being moved, exactly as they happen.
Your assistant knows the moment you're watching, and it narrates naturally
as it works.

Click **Hide assistant screen** any time to switch back to the avatar; the
work carries on either way.

<Note>
  If the button shows *"Assistant desktop is starting up…"*, the machine is
  still waking. Calls connect voice-first so you're never kept waiting to
  talk — the screen option unlocks moments later, mid-call, with no
  reconnect.
</Note>

## Taking the controls

Watching is read-only by default — your clicks don't touch anything. To
interact, use the second toggle that appears once the screen is showing:
**Enable mouse & keyboard control**.

Now your pointer and keystrokes go straight to the assistant's machine.
You can open things, type, demonstrate, fix, or explore — it's a shared
computer between the two of you, and you have the wheel:

- **Your assistant steps back.** It knows you've taken control and won't
  fight you for the mouse. It watches what you do, and you can talk through
  it together on the call — *"see, this is the section that needs checking
  first"* is a powerful way to [teach it](/learning/teaching).
- **Hand back any time.** Click **Disable mouse & keyboard control** to
  return to view-only. Your assistant picks the work back up, taking a
  fresh look at the screen to account for anything you changed.

This is true joint work on one machine — you drive while it watches, it
drives while you watch, swapping as often as you like within the call.

## The two screen shares, side by side

It's worth keeping the directions straight, because a Meet call offers both:

| Control | Whose screen | Who can act on it |
|---|---|---|
| **Share your screen** | Yours | Only you — your assistant can *see* it and guide you, never click it |
| **Show assistant screen** | The assistant's | The assistant — and **you too**, once you enable mouse & keyboard control |

The asymmetry is deliberate: your machine stays yours. The assistant's
machine is the shared workbench.

## Good habits

- **Watch the first run of anything new.** A newly taught workflow or a
  first [task run](/tasks/overview) is the perfect time to open the screen
  and confirm it's doing what you meant.
- **Demonstrate instead of describing.** For fiddly procedures, take
  control and do it once while narrating — your assistant
  [learns from what it sees](/learning/teaching).
- **Never type secrets on its screen.** Passwords and API keys belong in
  the secure [Integrations flows](/integrations/permissions), not typed
  into a shared desktop — your assistant will never ask you to.
